**Figure 1 | Individual rates of adolescent cortical thinning track genetic risk and symptoms of adolescent-onset mental illness.**
CT and ΔCT are the intercept and slope of one linear mixed model on the per-scan cortex-wide mean: CT is thickness at the sample-mean scan age (12.8 years), not at the first scan, and ΔCT is the per-year change (negative = thinning). Both are fitted from all of a child's scans. Per-child estimates are shrunk toward the mean (ΔCT SD 2.3 µm/yr against a model between-child SD of 4.2 µm/yr), and CT and ΔCT are nearly independent across children (r = 0.10).

- **a** Study design. Cortex-wide mean thickness from 26,946 scans of 8,716 children (brain silhouettes are schematic, not data) enters one linear mixed model with a random intercept and age slope per child, giving each child's CT intercept and ΔCT slope. Polygenic scores built from published GWAS are tested against each trait (e), each trait against symptoms at 15–17 years adjusted for baseline symptoms (f, g), and the score → ΔCT → symptom path by mediation (h, i).
- **b** Age at scan by visit; 8,716 children, 26,946 scans.
- **c** Children by number of QC-passing scans: 3,003 / 3,508 / 2,205 with 4 / 3 / 2 scans enter the slope model; 1,467 children with a single scan pass QC but are not modelled (a slope needs at least two scans).
- **d** Per-scan cortical mean thickness against age for 250 random children (grey) and three highlighted children (points, colour), chosen for well-separated slopes: a fast thinner (ΔCT -24.8 µm/yr), a near-average child (-19.6) and a slow thinner (-14.3), four scans each spanning at least 5.5 years. Each coloured line is that child's fitted trajectory from the mixed model in the title, fixed effects plus the child's random intercept and slope (site effect omitted), so its height at 12.8 years is the child's CT and its slope is their ΔCT. Lines are shrunk toward the population trajectory, most for children with fewer or closely spaced scans.
- **e** PRS-CS polygenic score association with ΔCT (purple) and CT (green) on each row; β per SD of score with 95% CI; filled = p < 0.05. Schizophrenia (2025 GWAS): ΔCT p = 0.01 EUR / 0.004 pooled, CT p = 0.30 / 0.81. C+T and SBayesRC give the same ΔCT pattern; bipolar and ADHD scores are in SI. * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001 (uncorrected).
- **f** Symptoms at ages ~15–17 (mean of the available waves 5–7) against the same single-LMM traits (ΔCT purple, CT green; each fitted alone; filled = p < 0.05; * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001). Model: y_late ~ trait + y_baseline + age_late + sex + site (OLS, family-clustered SEs), β in SD of y_late per SD of trait, so each estimate is the association with later symptoms beyond the child's baseline level (not a difference score or a symptom slope). CBCL parent report, log1p raw scale sums; p-factor is the first principal component of the eight syndrome scales. Traits are signed as in e (ΔCT negative = faster thinning), so β < 0 means more symptoms with faster thinning or with thinner cortex. n = 8,189. Faster thinning goes with more depressive (β -0.032, p = 0.003), externalising (p = 0.03) and general (p-factor, p = 0.05) symptoms; CT shows none of these. CBCL thought problems and youth-reported psychotic-like experiences (PQ-BC) are in SI. Exploratory, uncorrected.
- **g** The strongest association in f at the child level: DSM-oriented depressive score at 15–17 years, residualised on the baseline score, age, sex and site (SD of the late score), against ΔCT (8,181 children; points, means and 95% CIs in equal-width ΔCT bins of 1 µm/yr (269–1,580 children per bin; the 465 children beyond the outer bin edges are omitted from the bins but included in the fit); purple, OLS fit on all children, p = 0.007 with family-clustered SEs). The slope here (-0.027 SD per SD of ΔCT) is slightly smaller than in f (-0.032) because ΔCT itself is not residualised on the covariates.
- **h, i** Mediation of each polygenic score's association with later symptoms through ΔCT (pooled arm, 8,076 and 8,072 children), each for the outcome in f on which that score has its largest total effect (externalising symptoms for schizophrenia, depressive symptoms for depression). Product of coefficients with the covariates of f plus 10 genetic PCs: a, score → ΔCT (ΔCT in SD; negative = faster thinning); b, ΔCT → symptoms with the score in the model; c′, the direct effect; c, the total effect. The indirect effect a·b, with a 95% CI from 1000 bootstrap replicates resampling families, is +0.0006 for schizophrenia (3.3% of the total) and +0.0010 for depression (1.3%). Because ΔCT is a shrunk per-child estimate, a·b is attenuated by its reliability R (mean 0.30; 0.17 / 0.32 / 0.38 for children with 2 / 3 / 4 scans). Dividing by R gives the reliability-corrected values shown in grey, +0.0021 for schizophrenia (11% of the total) and +0.0031 for depression (4%); this approximate correction treats R as known and the same for every child. a·b > 0 with both a and b negative: higher scores, faster thinning, more symptoms. * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001. All outcomes and both arms are in SI.

Methods
- ABCD release 7.0, HCP-MMP1.0 (358 parcels, cortex-wide mean thickness per scan); 8,716 children with >= 2 QC-passing scans.
- Slope model: thickness ~ age + sex + (1 + age | child) + (1 | site), on the per-scan cortical mean (d–i); the trait is the child's age slope.
- PRS: PRS-CS shown (C+T and SBayesRC in SI); EUR arm scored with European discovery GWAS, pooled arm with multi-ancestry GWAS, z-scored within ancestry cluster; model score + age + sex + 10 PCs + (1 | family); filled = p < 0.05.
- ΔCT reliability by scans per child is in figS_dct_reliability.png.
- Score → symptom associations for all outcomes and both arms, and mediation through ΔCT for every outcome, are in figS_prs_symptoms.png.
- MAGMA gene-set tests (SCZ/MDD common-variant and exome sets, three LD references) are in Supplementary Information: no set is enriched for ΔCT once ABCD's own EUR children are the LD reference.
- Sensitivity analyses (DK parcellation, mean of per-parcel slopes, PGC3 2022 GWAS, SBayesR, per-ancestry strata) in Supplementary Information.
