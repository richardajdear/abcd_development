ABCD · Cortical development genetics · Pipeline v2

Does adolescent brain development share genetics with adolescent-onset illness?

A sibling-aware, ancestry-aware rebuild of the ABCD genetics pipeline. It corrects a real confound in the previous analysis, sharpens one result, weakens another — and leaves the central claim resting on two non-independent tests.

Sample 8,082 genotyped + phenotyped (4,116 European) Pipeline hpc_v2 · GENESIS PC-AiR/PC-Relate Compared against hpc v1 · GCTA fastGWA Updated 7 Sep 2026

The question

What we were testing

Five phenotypes derived from longitudinal cortical thickness in ABCD: baseline_thickness (a positive control), global_slope (cortex-wide thinning rate — the pre-specified primary phenotype), and three principal components of the regional slope. The hypothesis is that the genetic architecture of adolescent cortical thinning overlaps with genetic risk for disorders that emerge in adolescence — schizophrenia and major depression.

Three instruments test that overlap, and they differ in what limits them. Polygenic scores draw their power from the discovery side, where PGC has hundreds of thousands of cases, so they work at our sample size. MAGMA aggregates our SNP signal to genes and asks whether disorder-implicated genes are enriched. Genetic correlation needs both traits to be well-powered, and ours is not.

Method

What changed between v1 and v2

Same subjects, same phenotypes, same disorder GWAS, and — deliberately — the same downstream code: v1's own MAGMA and LDSC scripts were pointed at the v2 summary statistics, so any difference is the association model and nothing else.

What differs is how relatedness and ancestry are estimated. ABCD deliberately recruited siblings, and it is ancestrally diverse. A conventional genetic relatedness matrix confuses those two things: two people look genetically similar whether they are cousins or simply share ancestry. v1 built both its ancestry covariates and its association model on such a matrix. v2 replaces it with PC-AiR (ancestry axes computed on unrelated individuals, relatives projected onto them) and PC-Relate (kinship estimated conditional on those axes).

Sample size is not the difference — v1's association step already kept siblings. v2 buys a cleaner separation of kinship from ancestry, nothing more.

Within Europeans the two pipelines agree to within noise on every λ — as they should, because inside one continental group there is little ancestry structure to disentangle from kinship. v1's inflation was confounding, not misspecification

v1 recorded an LDSC intercept of 1.0995 ± 0.0085 on the pooled scan and attributed it to European LD scores being applied to a multi-ancestry sample — a modelling artefact rather than population structure. v2's scan is equally multi-ancestry and used the same LD scores. Its intercept is 1.0036 ± 0.0060.

If the cause were LD-score misspecification, the intercept would not have moved. It was structure, and the new relatedness model removed it. The same conclusion arrives from the other direction: an in-sample LD panel returned strongly negative heritability on v1's statistics (down to −0.495, z = −8.0) and behaves normally on v2's.

Consequence: v1's 20 genome-wide hits for baseline_thickness do not survive. They attenuate to p = 8.2e−08 (chr2) and 9.5e−08 (chr12) — still the top two signals genome-wide, so not ancestry artefacts, but no longer significant.

Is SCZ × global_slope significant? No — and the ± is why

The value −0.149 ± 0.109 reports a standard error, not a confidence-interval half-width. That gives z = −1.37 and p = 0.171; the 95% interval is [−0.363, +0.064]. To reach p < 0.05 two-sided at that standard error, rg would have to be ≤ −0.214.

A directional prior does license a one-sided test. Even then nothing clears 0.05 except v1's pooled arm — the one specification now known to have been structure-confounded. The only "significant" cell is the artefact v2 removed.

There is a further reason the prior-hypothesis argument does not rescue this. A polygenic-score association and a genetic correlation estimate the same parameter — the genetic covariance between SCZ liability and thinning rate — from the same two datasets. The PRS result cannot be used to lower the bar for the rg and then have the rg reported as corroboration; that is one piece of evidence counted twice. The same applies to the consistent sign across the six specifications above: they share the ABCD sample entirely and the SCZ sample almost entirely.

Defensible to write: the genetic correlation is negative in every specification (−0.12 to −0.20), consistent in direction with the polygenic-score association, and not significantly different from zero in any (best p = 0.17 two-sided). Not defensible: calling it significant, or independent confirmation.

Both disorder GWAS were themselves multi-ancestry

A mismatch neither pipeline had noticed: the SCZ statistics in use are the PGC3 primary release (European and East Asian), and MDD is the PGC MDD2025 div trans-ancestry release. The European-only SCZ file was already on disk, unused; the European MDD file has now been downloaded and checksum-verified. So no arm of this project, v1 or v2, had ever been ancestry-matched on both sides.

PRS

Polygenic scores, and the sibling test

Polygenic scoring does not use our GWAS — it applies PGC weights to our genotypes — so v2 leaves the population estimates untouched. What v2 adds is the decomposition that the sibling design uniquely permits: each child's score splits into a family mean and a deviation from it. The deviation is identified only by which alleles that child happened to inherit, which is random with respect to ancestry, upbringing and parental phenotype.

Alzheimer's negative control running

The hypothesis is specific to adolescent-onset illness, and that specificity is only demonstrated if a late-onset brain disorder fails the same test. PGC-ALZ2 (Wightman et al. 2021, excluding 23andMe) is being run through the identical rg, MAGMA and polygenic-score pipeline, with and without the APOE region. If Alzheimer's risk predicts adolescent cortical thinning as well as schizophrenia risk does, the schizophrenia result is far more likely to reflect generic brain-related polygenicity or residual stratification than anything developmental. Results will be added here.

The honest read

Are the PRS and MAGMA results convincing on their own?

Not yet. They are a coherent, directionally consistent set of weak signals — and every one of the concerns below is about the same underlying limitation, which is that the phenotype carries very little genetic signal to work with.

  1. The effect is at the edge of detectability. The SCZ polygenic score explains roughly 0.17% of variance in thinning rate. Effects that small are exactly where residual confounding lives.
  2. The two surviving results are not independent. The polygenic association and the MAGMA locus-pool enrichment both aggregate SCZ discovery signal against ABCD signal, on the same two datasets. They are two views of one measurement, not two measurements.
  3. The causal test came back uninformative. Within families, the SCZ score does not predict thinning (β = +0.007). At 18% power that is not evidence against the effect — but it means the population association has never been shown to survive the control that would distinguish a causal effect from stratification, assortative mating or genetic nurture.
  4. Multiple testing depends on pre-specification. Correcting across the eight score thresholds gives padj = 0.034, which holds only because global_slope was designated the primary phenotype in advance. The manifest does record it that way. Correct across all 160 disorder × phenotype × threshold × stratum cells and nothing survives.
  5. There is no replication. Not internally, not externally. Every number here comes from one sample.

What the evidence does support: a consistent, small, directionally stable association between schizophrenia genetic risk and faster adolescent cortical thinning, visible through two related instruments and not contradicted by a third. That is a promising lead worth pursuing. It is not yet a demonstrated shared genetic architecture.

Next

What would actually move this forward

The binding constraint is the phenotype's genetic signal: global_slope has h² ≈ 0.137 and an LDSC h² z of 1.16. Genetic correlation power, polygenic r², and gene-level power all scale with that number. Most of what follows attacks it.

Cheap, and uses data already on disk

  1. Finish the negative-control panel. Alzheimer's is running. Add an ASD score as a childhood-onset contrast — the sumstats are already in the same directory. A specificity gradient across onset age (childhood → adolescent → late-life) would be a far stronger argument than any single association, and it costs one job.
  2. Replace clumping-and-thresholding with PRS-CS or SBayesR. Typically 1.5–2× the variance explained for the same discovery data, which is the cheapest real power gain available. PRS-CSx would additionally do the ancestry-stratified arm properly.
  3. Test the locus-pool finding with local genetic correlation. The MAGMA result says the signal concentrates in SCZ loci. LAVA or SUPERGNOVA tests exactly that, is far better powered than genome-wide rg when signal is localised, and turns a gene-set enrichment into a proper statistical statement about shared architecture.
  4. Formalise the age × PRS interaction. The hypothesis is developmental. If schizophrenia risk acts on the rate of thinning, the interaction is the direct test, and it uses the longitudinal structure that makes this cohort valuable.

Moderate effort, larger payoff

  1. Multivariate regional GWAS. A single global slope discards the regional patterning that a spatial map of SCZ risk already appears to track. MOSTest across parcels has repeatedly found an order of magnitude more loci for cortical morphology than univariate scans, and it is the single most likely route to a phenotype with enough h² for the downstream tests to work.
  2. Improve the slope itself. More waves, explicit measurement-error modelling, and longitudinal harmonisation raise h² directly. Every downstream test inherits the gain.
  3. Get more sibling pairs. 1,339 pairs gives 18% power for the within-family test — the one test that could establish causality. Newer ABCD genotype releases add subjects, and the within-family estimate is the number worth buying.

The one that would settle it

Replication in an independent cohort with longitudinal cortical measures — IMAGEN, Generation R, ALSPAC, or the ABCD-adjacent lifespan samples. Everything above improves an estimate inside one dataset. Only an independent sample converts a lead into a finding, and at this effect size it is what a reviewer will ask for first.
